# TeamLeet — a team lead simulator

Godot 4.6, Russian and English (switch in-game). All UI built in code; the only assets are the Inter font (OFL, `fonts/`) and interface sounds by Kenney (CC0, `sounds/`), one cue per change on screen with a sound toggle in the top bar. Run with F5.

Play in the browser: https://aragastmatb.github.io/teamleet/ — every push to `main` rebuilds it (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`, single-threaded web export so it runs without special server headers).

2Leet sells software to banks; you are the new lead of Chainer, its internal developer platform team (tasks come from Irina, the Head of Platform; Taras from corporate IT; the leads of three product teams; and banks). A four-week probation as the new lead of a team of four. On day one Irina names three key projects; at the end comes an assessment that weighs results (mostly those projects) against an anonymous team survey Marina runs halfway and at the end. Tasks come from a shuffled deck each morning (key projects on fixed days); events add and remove cards. You assign them with a click and pair people up. You have three tokens of attention a day for managing — a one-on-one (they come with whatever's on their mind: exhaustion, a grievance, pride in their work, wanting to grow), dropping in on Irina (show a result alone or with whoever did it, warn about a slip, ask for a deadline move or a contractor), sitting with someone over their task, helping with a complication; past that you stay late and wear yourself out. Free people propose their own initiatives (tech debt, docs, automating the weekly report, studying, Vika's showcase), which change the decks or the people. People with a reason to refuse push back, and you pick how to talk them round: tell it straight, flatter, cite the boss, promise a breather, or insist. Flattery and citing the boss only work when true; a lie about the boss may come out that evening. Ending the day simulates the work: progress, quality, fatigue, mood and skill growth.

- Skills and motives are hidden and revealed by results, the pace of work, and one-on-ones. Skills vary a little each run.
- Energy: a normal 8-hour day on work someone can do barely tires them; late hours, work beyond them and being pushed do, and weekends put people back on their feet. People stay late for a deadline they won't otherwise make, each by their own character (Alex always, Gena never, Vika only if the boss will see it), when the queue has piled up, or when you ask them to; their card shows it during the day ("staying late today +2 h"), and the late hours count as work done.
- About one and a half times more work arrives than the team can do without staying late. You can turn requests down in a conversation with whoever brought them: "not this month" (it comes back later), "not ours", or "let Irina decide" (needs weight); Irina's own tasks only with weight; key projects never. Every no adds up with that person, and every third time they complain to Irina — Taras at once.
- Overload leads to sick leave, resentment to resignation; Vika's ambition can cost you your job.
- Tasks hit complications while they're being worked on (an old bug, missing access, scope creep, broken data, a client changing their mind…); your call changes scope, deadline or quality, and some calls depend on the owner's real skill.
- Roles aren't assigned: they emerge from the tasks someone ends up doing.
- Morning scenes grow out of what's happening: Alex and Vika argue over credit after working together, Dima confesses a mistake, Gena forgets a task at the bottom of his list, Irina asks about a deadline that's slipping, Taras wants to borrow someone. Choices leave consequences that come back later.
- Fridays: a report of hours against the 40-hour week (yours included — a lead who does the work gets told off), then Irina asks about the week's most visible success and worst failure. Answers are checked against the truth: claiming personal control only holds if you took part, blaming the owner only if they could do it and weren't overworked, "we were swamped" only if the report shows overtime.
- Weight with Irina (shown in the top bar as how much she listens to you) grows when you're visible — key projects delivered, credit taken, honest early warnings — and drops with slips and lies. It's spent to protect the team: refusing tasks she drops on you, getting a contractor. Every change is announced with its cause; Taras and Vika show by example how weight is made. Enough of it at the end means a promotion.
- Leadership style by Adizes (PAEI: results, order, change, people). Everything you do leans one way — doing work yourself and pushing toward P, warnings, plans and docs toward A, initiatives and refusing dumps toward E, one-on-ones and credit toward I. The numbers are hidden: Irina hints at what she values on day one and remarks on your week every Friday, and the person whose kind of lead it was warms up while someone who got none of what they need says so. The team adapts: lean P and they work faster but tire, A and fewer surprises, E and more initiatives, I and fewer grudges. The assessment shows your code (e.g. PaEi), the kind of lead it makes you, including Adizes' mismanagement styles, and counts how well it fits Irina.
- Stepping in: on a task in progress you can ask how it's going (one token) and steer it — speed it up at the cost of quality, slow it down for quality, cut it to the minimum, show your own way (helps only if you really know this better than they do), take it over yourself (half the work done is lost getting into it), or leave them to it. Most people tell you straight when they're behind; Alex won't admit it until it's obvious. How they take it depends on who they are.
- You have skills of your own (a former engineer for now: strong in tech, weak with people); they set how fast and how well you do anything yourself. Choosing a background comes later, together with Irina.
- Irina comes in four kinds, one per letter (`C.BOSSES`); every run uses `C.DEFAULT_BOSS` for now, picking her at the start comes later.
- Each person has their own letter and character: Alex is a workaholic who doesn't say he's tired until he drops, Gena guards his energy and says no early, Dima only volunteers after praise and takes hits harder, Vika chases anything new. People volunteer initiatives only with energy to spare, not on a timer.
- On a phone held upright (any window taller than it is wide) the game switches to a phone layout: one screen at a time — board (one column with a status switcher), team, chat — with tabs at the bottom and an unread count on the chat. The chat opens by itself when someone needs an answer and goes back once you reply; a task opens as a sheet from the bottom. Lists scroll with a finger from anywhere, buttons included.
- The screen is a tracker: the team on the left, a kanban board in the middle, and one chat on the right where every conversation happens and every answer is given. Only the Friday report and the assessment open as documents.
- Assessment outcomes: results and team (a leader people follow), results without the team (master of delegation), the team without results (the team's favourite, probation failed), neither (failed); plus workaholic, replaced by Vika, the team fell apart.

## Content you can edit without code
- `data/tasks.tsv` — the task deck: one row per task (category, who brings it, size, deadline, whether the boss sees it, copies in the deck, Russian and English text). Open it in Excel or Google Sheets, or any text editor; the game reads it on start.
- `docs/lore.md` — the world (2Leet, the Chainer platform team) and what's agreed; `docs/plan.md` — the expansion plan.

## Files (`teamlead/`)
- `content.gd` — people, tones, tasks, schedule; all hidden numbers.
- `scenes.gd` — morning scenes: when they trigger, choices, consequences.
- `rules.gd` — the engine, no UI; can be driven headless.
- `locale.gd` — all RU/EN text.
- `main.gd` — the UI.
